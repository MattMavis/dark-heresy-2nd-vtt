# Experience and Advancement

Experience is kept as a **history**, not as a number you edit. Awards are entries, spends are
entries, and the totals on the sheet are derived from them. The consequence is that what the
sheet says and what the character has actually bought cannot drift apart.

The Experience panel on the character sheet shows both histories. The GM can delete any single
row; players cannot.

## Spending

**Spend Experience** is player-facing. It prices every available advance against that
character's own aptitudes and will not let them skip a rank.

Cost depends on how many of the advance's two aptitudes the character has. Two matches is
cheapest, none is dearest.

### Influence is not bought

The book lists nine characteristics as advanceable and Influence is not among them, so it does
not appear in the spend list. Influence changes through play, not purchase.

## Prerequisites

Talent prerequisites are read from the talent itself and checked against the character, so the
buy list shows what they qualify for at a glance. Psychic powers work the same way — see
[[Playing a Psyker]].

**A prerequisite the system cannot parse never blocks a purchase.** It stays advisory. The books
phrase requirements inconsistently and the parser will not understand all of them, and the
system's view is that a parser failure should not stop a legitimate buy.

The GM can override the check entirely with a tickbox, for the cases where the table has agreed
something the rules do not.

## Awarding

**Award Experience** is GM-only. It awards to a single character or the whole party at once,
records a reason, and names the recipients before you confirm.

## What was carried over

Characters who predate the ledger were given a single opening award entry and a single opening
spend entry, so their available XP is unchanged and history builds from the next transaction.

Earlier spending is **not** itemised, and deliberately so: a character is granted so much for
free at creation that there is no way after the fact to tell a granted advance from a bought one.

Only player characters were touched. NPCs are statted directly rather than bought with
experience, so they were left alone.
